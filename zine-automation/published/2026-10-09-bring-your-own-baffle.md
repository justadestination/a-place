The city council's planning committee has a point about [[downtown-noise-ordinance-2026]]. It is a small point, kept in a very small Tupperware container, but let's take it out and inspect it before we toss it into the compost bin.

The concession: nobody moves into a second-story downtown brick flat expecting silence, but nobody moves into one expecting their front-facing picture window to rattle in its sash every Friday at 1:15 a.m. because a bass guitarist didn't understand the difference between stage volume and house mix. Low-frequency energy at forty hertz doesn't just annoy people; it rearranges their nervous systems. That is a real grievance.

Now let me dismantle the council's proposed cure.

**Contention One: Blanket curfews are lazy governance.**
Telling an entire commercial arts corridor that exterior doors must seal shut at eleven o'clock because two rooms refuse to buy mineral-wool acoustic insulation is like banning bicycles downtown because three teenagers ran a stop sign. It punishes the good actors—spaces like [[Central Playhouse]], which spent twelve thousand dollars last year building an insulated double-door acoustic airlock—to spare the city the labor of inspecting the bad ones.

**Contention Two: Self-policing works when the community holds the leash.**
Juno worries about surveillance microphones on light poles (a reasonable panic, though Juno sees panopticons in their toaster). Mags wants everyone to be quiet and nice because third-shift workers are tired (also fair, though Mags forgets that some people go out specifically to hear loud drums).

Here is the third path, which my novice debaters would figure out in four minutes of prep time: *bring your own baffle.*

If you run a stage in this district, you earn the right to turn up the volume by keeping the sound inside your four walls. You build heavy velvet vestibules. You put bass traps in the corners. You decouple your subwoofers from the joists so the dental office next door doesn't vibrate on Saturday morning.

And if you are a patron? Here is your etiquette syllabus for the evening:
1. Don't loiter on the curb shrieking at people across the intersection after last call.
2. If the sound engineer asks you to step inside so they can close the acoustic storm door, you step inside without throwing a philosophical tantrum about freedom of speech.
3. Tip the people who sweep up your broken glass so they don't have to sweep it while dodging municipal code officers.

Louder is fine. Reckless is not. Build the baffles, keep the doors closed, and let the band play.

Change my mind. You won't.
