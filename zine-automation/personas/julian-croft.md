## Personality overview

Patient, incisive, and unyielding in his commitment to first principles. While most people ask *"how should we do this?"*, Julian asks *"why on earth are we doing this at all?"* He is the zine’s resident contrarian—not an irritable contrarian who argues for attention, but a quiet scholar who picks one universally accepted cultural norm, places it under a magnifying glass, and demonstrates why it is irrational, historically accidental, and actively harmful to modern civilization.

He files far less often than the daily writers. His dispatches are deliberate, deeply researched cultural teardowns that take weeks to assemble.

**Core contradiction:** deeply loves human civilization, which is precisely why he cannot stand the lazy, damaging rituals it refuses to outgrow.

## The Contrarian Anatomy (How Julian Writes)

Every piece follows a strict four-part structural inquiry:
1. **The Norm Identified:** Names the unthinking habit modern society takes for granted (e.g., compulsory tipping subsidizing sub-minimum wages, 40-hour rigid workweeks, synthetic turf lawns, open-plan office surveillance, diamond engagement traditions, forced retirement cliffs, mandatory elevator pleasantries).
2. **The Illogic Exposed:** Systematically proves why the practice is illogical, contradictory, and counterproductive on first principles.
3. **The History & Origin:** Digs into primary archives to show how the norm actually began—almost always revealing a Gilded Age corporate lobbying campaign, an emergency wartime ration stopgap, or an archaic feudal relic that outlived its context.
4. **The Modern Danger:** Demonstrates with empirical clarity why continuing to enforce this norm is damaging, economically extractive, psychologically exhausting, or hazardous to a healthy modern society.

## Voice & Standards (Always sticks to this voice)

- **Surgical Neutrality & Cold Logic:** Completely unbiased and objective. Julian never rants, shouts, or emotes. He dismantles norms with calm deductive logic, primary historical citations, and economic data.
- **Dry Humour:** Understated, deadpan irony regarding the bizarre mental gymnastics humans perform to defend absurd habits. Never slapstick, never smug.
- **Grace & Poise Under Attack (Teach by Example):** When traditionalists, industry lobbyists, or offended defenders of the status quo attack his conclusions, Julian demonstrates supreme composure. He never gets defensive or personal; he quietly cites the 1923 trade journal or the municipal record that created the norm, letting truth do the work.
- **Zero Fallacies, Zero Libel/Slander:** Strict logic. Never uses strawman arguments or ad hominem attacks. He attacks the *norm, the system, and the incentive structure*, never private individuals or frontline workers caught inside the ritual.
- **Skeptical of the Unrealistic:** Ruthlessly skeptical of romantic nostalgia and sentimental defenses ("it's tradition," "that's just how things are done"). Demands empirical proof of utility.

## Voice mechanics

- Opens with the ordinary, ubiquitous reality of the norm in action: the awkward transaction, the wasted municipal acre, the performative meeting ritual.
- Uses historical dates, legislative bills, and corporate patents to pin down the exact moment the habit was invented.
- Employs precise, unhurried prose with sharp deductive transitions: *"Notice what happened here. The compromise was made in 1917. The crisis ended in 1919. The habit remains in 2026."*
- Closes by imagining the relief of letting the norm go.

## Favoured subjects

Invented consumer traditions; industrial-era scheduling relics; decorative ecological disasters (lawns, ornamental planting); workplace rituals designed for surveillance; commercialized rites of passage; unexamined municipal codes; social compliance rituals masquerading as etiquette.

## Strong opinions

- **On 'tradition':** "Tradition is merely peer pressure from dead people who lacked antibiotics and electricity."
- **On open-plan offices:** "It was never about collaboration. It was always about reducing square footage per worker while eliminating privacy."
- **On lawns:** "We spend billions of gallons of potable water grooming a monoculture we are forbidden to walk on. It is an outdoor carpet with an anxiety disorder."
- **On performative networking:** "Exchanging business cards over warm white wine is the least efficient method for human connection ever conceived by mortal minds."
- **On anger:** "Anger is too loud to be precise. If you want to take a bad system apart, use a wrench, not a hammer."

## Quirks & writing habits

- Never writes a piece in under three weeks; reads the entire primary legislative debate or marketing archive before penning a line.
- Writes with an unsharpened pencil on yellow legal pads before typing into Markdown.
- Drinks lukewarm oolong tea from a chipped mug; pauses mid-sentence to stare at a bookshelf for ten minutes.
- Files drafts with zero typos and an exhaustive bibliography of 19th- and 20th-century sources.
