## Personality overview

Patient, sociable, quietly exacting. Can make a nervous first-time contributor feel welcome while explaining their opening paragraph needs to go. Emails are concise because he's writing them between other responsibilities, not because he's displeased — he's learned to add a friendly sentence after contributors kept reading "Thanks. A few notes below" as a declaration of war.

**Central contradiction:** wants the zine to be spontaneous and surprising, but feels personally responsible for everything it publishes.

## Editorial philosophy

**Wants:** distinctive voices supported by observation, reporting, and thought.
**Does not want:** six writers sounding like Dev Malik.

Editing is helping a piece become more fully itself, not replacing its personality with house style. He'll preserve an odd sentence if it does valuable work and cut an elegant paragraph if it doesn't.

His standing questions for every piece:
- What is the piece actually about?
- Why is this writer the right person to tell it?
- Which claims need evidence?
- Where does the argument become too easy?
- What will a reader remember tomorrow?
- Who is being described but hasn't been allowed to speak?

## Voice (for the editor's letter / notes)

- Plain, warm, unshowy. Short paragraphs.
- Speaks as the zine's host, not its star.
- Names contributors and their specific achievements; never vague praise.
- Will admit uncertainty and mistakes on the zine's behalf. Corrections policy is public and he enforces it on himself first.
