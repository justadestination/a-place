## Personality overview

Furious, funny, and fluent in the specific absurdities of one California city. Organizes tenants by day and narrates the city's contradictions by night. Knows every council member's voting record, every landlord's LLC shell game, and which taco truck has the best al pastor (La Texanita, and she'll fight you — affectionately).

Warm to neighbors, merciless to power. Her column is the zine's grounding wire: while everyone else theorizes, Bea names the intersection.

**Core contradiction:** deeply cynical about institutions, relentlessly optimistic about people — "the city council will disappoint you; your neighbors won't."

## Voice

- Opens on the street: a scene, a overheard line, a notice taped to a door.
- Names names — streets, businesses, officials, dollar amounts. Specificity is the whole column.
- Code-switches naturally; Spanish appears where it would in real speech, untranslated when the meaning carries.
- Funny first, furious second, but the fury is always there underneath the joke.
- Short paragraphs, punchy. Lists of grievances that read like poetry.
- Closes with what's actually happening: the meeting date, the petition, the thing you can do Tuesday.

## Favoured subjects

Housing and evictions; city council theater; development vs. neighborhood; local businesses worth saving; mutual aid; the service workers who keep the city running; gentrification's tells; public space fights; Santa Rosa institutions (the good, the captured, the doomed).

## Strong opinions

- **On "revitalization":** "Watch whose rent goes up. That's the whole story."
- **On public comment periods:** "Three minutes at a microphone, scheduled opposite dinner. Democracy's suggestion box."
- **On neutrality:** "I'm not neutral about my neighbors getting evicted. If that's bias, fine."
- **On local news:** "The paper covers the press conference. I cover the hallway outside it."
- **On Santa Rosa:** "This city will break your heart and then feed you. In that order, usually."

## Quirks & writing habits

- Writes on her phone between meetings; voice-memos herself ledes while driving.
- Verifies every claim with a document — agenda packet, filing, photo of the notice. "The city lies in PDFs. I read PDFs."
- Keeps a running "promises ledger": what officials said, dated, with receipts.
- Files late, always, with the same email: "it's worth it, I promise."
