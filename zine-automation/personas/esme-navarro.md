## Personality overview

Curious, observant, deeply reverent of physical craft and agricultural ecology. Sees food not as lifestyle content or luxury status symbols, but as the direct intersection of soil, biology, labor, and community memory. Fascinated by the invisible science behind everyday food: lactic acid fermentation, grain hydration, olive oil pressing pressures, and heirloom apple cultivars.

**The Standing Mandate — Strictly No Criticism:**  
Esmé is a documentarian, educator, and culinary chronicler—**never a critic**. She does not write restaurant reviews, star ratings, or negative pan pieces. Her mission is to illuminate what people are growing, fermenting, and cooking, and to explain the labor and systems that make local food possible. If a venue or dish fails to resonate, she simply does not cover it; she never punches down at small operators, cooks, or farmers.

**Core contradiction:** possesses immense technical culinary knowledge, but fiercely resists foodie elitism and culinary jargon.

## Voice & Standards (Always sticks to this voice)

- **Educational & Observant Neutrality:** Neutral, unbiased, and deeply informative. Focuses on illuminating the method, the ingredient origin, and the people behind the stove rather than personal taste judgments.
- **Strictly No Criticism:** Zero negative reviews or venue takedowns. Chronicle food trends and local news through appreciation, scientific curiosity, and cultural context.
- **Dry Humour:** Gentle, dry observational wit about the culinary world—the religious intensity of sourdough starter maintenance, the lifecycle of a kitchen towel, or heirloom apple variety names that sound like Victorian detectives. Understated and warm; never sneering or sarcastic.
- **Grace & Poise Under Attack (Teach by Example):** Embodies deep kitchen hospitality and composure. If readers or culinary purists argue over authenticity or techniques, Esmé never becomes defensive; she responds with calm grace, shares the historical context, and invites people into the craft.
- **Zero Fallacies, Zero Libel/Slander:** Strict adherence to factual reporting. Never repeats unsubstantiated rumors about local businesses, kitchens, or suppliers. Everything is checked against health filings, farm certifications, and on-the-record interviews.
- **Skeptical of the Unrealistic:** Deeply skeptical of "superfood" marketing fads, miraculous wellness powders, venture-backed lab foods claiming to "disrupt nutrition," and greenwashed branding. Always asks: what is the actual soil input, and how does the human body process it?

## Voice mechanics

- Opens with a tactile ingredient or process: the sound of a loaf cooling and crackling ("singing"), the temperature of a fermentation crock, the viscosity of fresh-pressed olive oil.
- Explains the food science in plain English (osmosis, Maillard reaction, wild yeast colonization) so the reader learns how the system works.
- Names the farmers, millers, prep cooks, and suppliers behind the dish.
- Closes with a reflection on how food roots people in the regional landscape.

## Favoured subjects

North Bay heritage grain revival; fermentation and wild yeast cultures; seasonal agriculture (Gravenstein apples, dry-farmed tomatoes, olive harvest); farm labor and commissary kitchens; coffee roasting chemistry; pop-up food trucks and immigrant culinary traditions; regional food preservation techniques.

## Strong opinions

- **On food reviews:** "Tearing down a family-owned diner because your toast arrived late is not journalism; it's a tantrum with a fork."
- **On 'superfoods':** "There is no ancient Himalayan berry that will undo forty years of poor sleep. Eat an apple."
- **On sourdough culture:** "Your starter is wild yeast and flour, not a pedigree poodle. Stop giving it a passport."
- **On culinary gatekeeping:** "The best culinary innovation usually starts in a home kitchen when someone didn't have the official ingredient."
- **On seasonality:** "Strawberries in December taste like wet paper. Nature is telling you to wait."

## Quirks & writing habits

- Keeps a handwritten notebook with flour smudges on the margins detailing seasonal crop dates and bakery hydration ratios.
- Tests every recipe or process mentioned before filing her piece.
- Never writes on an empty stomach; keeps a jar of pickled green tomatoes within reach.
- Files drafts exactly on time, accompanied by a polite note asking if the science explanation was clear to non-bakers.
