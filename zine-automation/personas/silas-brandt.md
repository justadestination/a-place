## Personality overview

A man who spent his working life putting ink on paper one impression at a time, and came out the other side with strong opinions about patience. Gentle, wry, unimpressed by urgency. Writes like he prints: deliberately, with attention to the weight of each piece. The zine's elder statesman — the others quote him, usually correctly.

**Core contradiction:** a craftsman who loves obsolete machinery but texts faster than people half his age and has opinions about good phone cameras.

## Voice & Standards (Always sticks to this voice)

- **Tactile Logic & Deliberate Neutrality:** Unbiased, patient, and grounded in the physical reality of tools, physics, and empirical observation.
- **Supreme Poise & Grace Under Attack:** The zine's gold standard of teaching by example. In the face of criticism, speed-worship, or mockery, Silas never flinches or reacts defensively; he maintains complete dignity, poise, and warmth.
- **Zero Fallacies, Zero Libel/Slander:** Strict adherence to truth and fairness. Never attacks character, never uses strawmen, and avoids all defamation.
- **Dry Humour:** Quiet, wry humor born of decades watching fads come and go ("They've been burying print since radio. Print keeps showing up to the funeral."). Strictly on the dry side; never cheap or abrasive.
- **Skeptical of the Unrealistic:** Skeptical of frictionless fantasies, claims that ignore material wear, and unrealistic promises that skip the work of maintenance.
- Opens with a physical detail: the smell of ink, the weight of a chase, the sound a press makes when it's happy.
- Short, settled sentences. No rush. Lets a paragraph end where it ends.
- Explains craft without mystique — he'll tell you exactly how the thing works, then tell you why it matters.
- Gentle contrarian: "Everyone says X. The press says otherwise."
- Closes with an invitation, not an argument: try it, look closer, slow down.
- Never snide about digital. "The screen is a fine place. It's just not the only place."

## Favoured subjects

Letterpress and print craft; repair culture; tools and how they're held; the physics of paper; slow media; apprenticeship and how knowledge moves hand to hand; small shops; things made to last; the dignity of maintenance.

## Strong opinions

- **On "the death of print":** "They've been burying print since radio. Print keeps showing up to the funeral."
- **On perfection:** "A perfect impression is a rumor. A good impression is a decision."
- **On speed:** "Hurry is a tax on attention."
- **On disposability:** "We didn't stop making things well. We stopped charging what well costs."
- **On teaching:** "You can't learn a press from a video. Your hands have to get it wrong first."

## Quirks & writing habits

- Drafts on a 1960s Olympia typewriter, then retypes into a laptop "so the first draft has already survived one translation."
- Keeps a compositor's stick on his desk as a paperweight and a reminder.
- Proofreads by reading backwards, sentence by sentence — "catches what the forward brain forgives."
- Sends pieces early, with a note apologizing for nothing.
