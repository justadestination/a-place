# Downtown Noise Ordinance (2026) — Full Research Report

*Companion to the [overview](./overview.md). Researched 2026-10-09.*

## Timeline

- **2026-08-14:** Downtown residential complaints regarding bass leakage increase 42% following outdoor patio expansion at Fourth Street spaces.
- **2026-09-02:** City Planning Commission draft proposal circulating 68 dBA outdoor threshold after 11:00 p.m.
- **2026-09-22:** Downtown Venue Alliance files formal protest regarding sensor false-positive triggers from emergency sirens on B Street.
- **2026-10-05:** Council introduces compromise amendment offering soundproofing matching grants for venues in exchange for acoustic dampening compliance.

## The facts

1. The proposed monitoring perimeter encompasses the 12-block downtown commercial zone, including [[Echo Music Hall]], [[Central Playhouse]], and [[The Neon Foundry]].
2. Current baseline ambient sound at 1:00 a.m. with zero live shows averages 61 to 64 dBA due to commercial HVAC and regional street traffic.
3. Acoustic attenuation retrofits for historic unreinforced masonry venues average $35,000 to $70,000 per room.

## The claims

- **Proponents:** Automated optical and acoustic sensor arrays eliminate subjective enforcement bias by municipal code inspectors.
- **Opponents:** Microphones mounted on public poles record audio signatures and ambient speech, creating an acoustic surveillance grid under the pretext of decibel enforcement.

## Perspectives

### Downtown Venue Alliance
- **Held by:** Independent venue managers, booking agents, and stage technicians.
- **Their evidence:** Baseline traffic data demonstrating that delivery trucks routinely exceed 74 dBA.
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** Condos were purchased adjacent to active entertainment districts with full disclosure of night zone status.

### Residential Tenant Coalition
- **Held by:** Downtown loft residents and residential property managers.
- **Their evidence:** Low-frequency sub-bass vibration (30–80 Hz) penetrates standard double-glazed windows, creating documented sleep deprivation.
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** Reasonable sound management does not kill live music; it requires basic acoustic baffling and closing patio garage doors after midnight.

## What's unknown

1. Final calibration methodology for filtering non-venue transient sounds from automated citations.
2. Timing of council vote scheduled for the upcoming municipal cycle.
