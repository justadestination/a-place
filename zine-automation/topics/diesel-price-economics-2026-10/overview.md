# Diesel price economics

## What happened

Diesel prices have risen sharply in the past year, with the cost of the fuel roughly doubling and pressures continuing into October 2026. BBC Verify reported that both the war in Ukraine and the US-Israeli war with Iran have cut global diesel exports, but the fall from Gulf states, hit by the Iran war, has been the larger of the two. The US president has blamed Ukraine's strikes on Russian refineries; a subsequent deal to import diesel from Russia was presented as a way to lower prices for Americans.

Heating oil has been hit hardest in real terms. The BBC reported that prices for heating oil customers "near[ly] doubled in the past year," prompting a government support package worth £53m for low-income families and a review of minimum order volumes of 500 litres.

## Why it matters

Diesel is the feedstock and fuel for the lorries, vans and ships that form the backbone of global supply chains. A sustained increase in diesel costs feeds directly into the price of goods, from grocery deliveries to building materials. For a zine that covers small shops, repair culture and the cost of living, the story is a concrete example of how a conflict half a world away shows up in a local price tag — and how a political promise ("lower prices for Americans") collides with physical supply constraints.

## What the main sides say

**The Trump administration's framing:** the president has blamed Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries for the global reduction in diesel exports, urging Zelensky to "stop" and suggesting Ukraine "get a new leader who can make a deal."

**The BBC Verify evidence:** the figures point at the Iran war as the bigger driver of reduced Gulf exports. The UK government has promised tighter regulation of the heating oil sector, accepting the Competition and Markets Authority's recommendation, but has so far offered no price cap.

**Campaigners' framing:** the CMA found 1,700 households were forced to "re-order at significantly higher prices or go without fuel" after the war broke out, costing some up to £350.

## What's still unknown

Whether the Russia-diesel import deal actually lowers prices, and by how much; whether the government's promised regulatory regime takes effect; and whether the heating oil minimum-order review translates into cheaper bills.
