## The facts

- Diesel prices rose sharply through the past year. BBC Verify cites figures showing both the Ukraine and Iran conflicts significantly reduced global diesel exports; the fall in supplies from Gulf states due to the Iran war was the greater of the two.
- The US president blamed Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries for the reduction, and suggested Ukraine "get a new leader who can make a deal."
- Following the war, the US announced a deal to import diesel from Russia, promising "lower prices for Americans."
- The UK government announced a support package worth £53m for low-income families who use heating oil, distributed by local authorities.
- A CMA inquiry found 1,700 households were forced to "re-order at significantly higher prices or go without fuel" after the war, costing them up to £350.
- The government said it would work with industry to "develop a regulatory regime for the heating oil market, protecting consumers and promoting competition." Minimum order levels — set for some customers at 500 litres — will be reviewed; domestic tanks typically hold between 1,000 and 2,000 litres.
- The UK, US and Wales all operate variable gas and electricity tariffs, but no equivalent price cap for heating oil had been proposed as of the BBC report.

## The claims

- "**Lower prices for Americans**" — the US president's stated aim of the Russia-diesel deal (attributed quote).
- "**Both conflicts matter, but Iran is bigger**" — BBC Verify's figure-based assessment (attributed to the report).
- "**Prices near double in a year**" — BBC, on heating oil.
- "**The government promised a cap**" — not supported; the government promised a regulatory regime and a review, not a cap.
- "**The CMA found 1,700 households harmed**" — stated directly in the BBC reporting.

## Perspectives

### The US administration's framing
- **Held by:** the US president and the White House.
- **Their evidence:** Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and the resulting disruption to global supply.
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** the BBC Verify report does not dispute strikes on refineries; it contests the relative scale, finding the Iran war's effect on Gulf exports larger.

### The BBC Verify analysis
- **Held by:** BBC Verify (Ben Chu, Policy and analysis correspondent).
- **Their evidence:** figures on diesel export volumes from conflicting manufacturers.
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** the relative size of the Iran-driven fall in Gulf exports versus the Ukraine-driven fall on Russian product.

### The UK government's framing
- **Held by:** ministers (Energy minister Polly Billington quoted in the BBC).
- **Their evidence:** consumer complaints and the CMA inquiry; an intention to "develop a regulatory regime."
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** the claim that there is no help at all; a support package and a review exist.

### Campaigners / the CMA
- **Held by:** the Competition and Markets Authority and heating oil campaigners.
- **Their evidence:** 1,700 households re-ordered at higher prices or went without fuel.
- **What they say the other side gets wrong:** that the cost-of-living pressure is temporary or cosmetic; the figures point at structural supply shock.

## What's unknown

- Whether the Russia-diesel import deal produced lower prices, and what those prices actually were.
- Whether the promised regulatory regime will be introduced or simply announced.
- Whether the heating oil minimum-order review will lower bills for households.
- Whether long-run diesel demand will grow enough to absorb the supply reductions.

## Glossary

- **Diesel** — a distillate fuel used by heavy transport and heating; a physical commodity whose price tracks crude and refinery supply.
- **CMA** — the UK's Competition and Markets Authority, the competition watchdog.
- **Wholesale** — price the refiner sells to a distributor, as distinct from the pump price.
- **Supply chain** — the chain of lorries, vans and ships that moves goods from factory to shop.
