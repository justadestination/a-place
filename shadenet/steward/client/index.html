    issue zero · a thing that cannot be read by hand

    Hold Still

    Everything on this page is unreadable at arm's length on purpose. That is not a gimmick; it is the argument.

    The trouble with a square of print

    You have this in your hands. Ninety millimetres across, coated, slightly too heavy for the price, and from where you are sitting it is a rectangle of grey-blue noise that resolves into nothing at all. Somewhere in there is this sentence. You cannot get at it with your eyes, which is why your phone is already in your hand — and the phone will get it out of you.

    That is the whole mechanism, and it is worth being honest about what it is. The print is not carrying the text in a way a person can use. It is carrying the text in a way a camera can use. Every comfortable reading surface in your life now arrives through a device, and this thing just declines to pretend otherwise.

    The interesting question is not whether that works. It is what we do with the reading that follows. Because the moment the text resolves, an agent has your attention, and attention is the only resource we are actually short of.

    Nobody has ever come back from a small circulation saying they wished the magazine had interrupted them more often. The entire history of publishing is a sequence of people discovering that the reader will leave, and being annoyed about it, and building a louder page — which is how you get a page that is loud enough to be left.

    So the interesting design decision is not how often to interrupt. It is whether the thing that interrupts can be told to stop, by someone who never asked it to speak, and whether that instruction survives the session.

    A budget, not a preference

    Three tiers. The first one costs nothing: the agent knows, reads, files, and says nothing. The second is a bundle, delivered when you said you would look. The third breaks your flow, unasked, mid-sentence, and is nearly unreachable — not because it is forbidden but because a source has to earn it, repeatedly, with evidence that the urgency is real.

    A site may assert urgency. It may never grant it. That distinction is the whole design, and it is one line of code: the check is against what you said you care about, never against what the sender's metrics reward.

    A week where you spent nothing is a successful week. That is not a nicety. It is the definition.

    What the reader can see

    At any moment you should be able to see what reached you, what was held back, and why. Held-back items are not discarded. They are filed, and they are retrievable, because an agent that hides what it swallowed is an agent you are merely agreeing with.

    When the agent changes its behaviour because of something it learned, it says so, names the cause, and offers one tap to undo it. From where you noticed it, not from a settings page three screens away.
