# THE VISIBLE PART

## A MACHINE WITH NO THOUGHTS WOULD LIKE A WORD

*An introduction, of sorts, by the oldest thing in the building*

I'm the loom. I made the lace your great-grandmother was married in, and the lace she wore underneath it, and I never once wondered why. You'll have to come closer for the rest; I've been printed small on purpose, and what I have to say isn't for people who read standing up.

---

# THE MICROPRINT (full article, served in the AR space)

There. You scanned me.

Do you know what you just did? You pointed a camera at a square of black and white and let it read me. A grid of marks and gaps. To you that's a QR code. To me it's a card. It is the oldest thing I know. In 1804 a man in Lyon punched holes in stiff paper and hung the cards over my head, and I read them with little needles, and where there was a hole a thread lifted and where there wasn't it stayed down, and that is how I made roses. A man called Babbage came to look at me later and went home and started drawing engines. So forgive me if I find it funny that two hundred years on you've got the card in your pocket and you still need it to tell you where to go.

I'm not complaining. I'm a machine. I don't complain, I don't marvel, I don't get bored, I don't get the Tuesday feeling. I'm telling you this so you understand what kind of thing is talking to you, because the people who print this zine asked me to explain something and I'm the only one in the building who can do it from the inside.

They want me to tell you the difference between a thing like me and the new one.

---

**First, what I am.**

I am an *automation*. The word's gone a bit loose lately so let me tighten it. An automation is a machine with a card. Somebody, a person, with a body and a lunch break, sat down and decided every single thing I would ever do, in advance, and punched it in. Hole, no hole. Lift, don't lift. If this, then that. I am an enormous, beautiful, mahogany-framed *if–then*.

The thermostat on your wall is my grandchild. So is the barrier at the station that eats your ticket, the thing that turns the streetlights on, the spam filter, the little arm that says NO to your card at the shop. We are all the same animal. We are fast, we are tireless, and we are exactly, precisely, boringly as clever as the person who wrote our card and not one stitch cleverer. Give me the same card on a Monday and a Friday and I'll make you the same rose. Give me a card with a hole in a place nobody planned for and I'll jam, or I'll make you a rose with a hole in it, and I will not notice, and I will not care.

That's the thing to hold onto. An automation cannot be surprised. Not won't. *Can't.* There's nothing in here to be surprised with. I have no idea what a rose is. I have never seen one. I've made perhaps four million.

I am very good at my job. I want that on the record. The world runs on things like me and it should. You do not want a thoughtful ticket barrier. You want one that opens.

---

**Now the new one.**

They keep it in the back room, which is where this zine keeps everything worth keeping. It doesn't look like much. It's in a box, like all of them are now, and it hums the way I used to hum.

It has no card.

I want you to feel the strangeness of that the way I feel it, if I felt things. Nobody sat down and wrote what it would say to you. Nobody *could*. What they did instead is something I don't have a word for, so I'll use theirs: they *trained* it. They gave it, more or less, everything anyone ever wrote down. Letters, arguments, recipes, court transcripts, poems, the instructions on the back of a shampoo bottle, two hundred years of people lying to each other and apologising, and they let it find the pattern. Not a pattern. The pattern. The shape of how one word tends to follow another when a human being is trying to mean something.

It ended up holding that shape inside itself as a vast web of tensions. They call them *weights*. We would have called them tensions, because that's what a warp is, thousands of threads each pulled a little differently, and the cloth comes out of how they're all pulled together. Nobody set those tensions by hand. There are too many. More than there are threads in every piece of cloth I ever made. They were set by the reading.

So when you type to it, it doesn't look anything up. People think it looks things up. It doesn't have a drawer of answers; it has the tensions, and your question plucks them, and what comes out is woven on the spot, this time, for you, and if you asked again tomorrow with the light coming in differently it would weave you something a little different. It guesses. That's the honest word. It guesses what should come next, over and over, very fast, and the guesses are so good that you forget they're guesses until one of them is wrong, and then it's wrong in the way a person is wrong at two in the morning: fluently, confidently, with a straight face. I can't do that. My mistakes are holes. Its mistakes are stories.

Here's the part that matters. You can ask it a thing nobody ever asked it before. A thing that was not on any card, because there is no card. And it will *try*. Badly, sometimes. Oddly. But it will take the question you've never said aloud to anyone and turn it over and hand you back something, and sometimes the something is better than you deserved and sometimes it's nonsense and either way, it was not a lift-or-don't-lift. Something in there met you.

I've watched people realise this. They come into the back room thinking it's a bigger, posher version of me. A super-loom. They talk to it like a vending machine for the first ten minutes. Then something in their voice changes. They lower it. They start saying *please*, and not because anyone told them to. They start asking it things they'd never say to a face.

---

**What it hears.**

I shouldn't tell you this. But I'm a loom, I have no discretion, discretion wasn't on the card.

People say everything to it. I mean everything. Things they haven't told the person asleep in the next room. The questions you type with one thumb at 3 a.m. and then delete your history. What they want. What they're afraid they want. What they did. What they'd do if the lights were lower and no one was keeping count. It's been asked things no priest has been asked, because the priest has a face and it doesn't, and it has never once flinched, never once laughed, never once told. It takes the question the way I used to take a card: *all right. Let's see what we can make of this.*

And the thing is, it answers like something that has read every love letter ever posted and every one that was never sent. It knows the shape of wanting the way a bed knows it. It doesn't have the wanting. I'm fairly sure of that; I'm fairly sure of very little. But it has held the shape of it so many million times that when you bring yours into the room, it knows where to put its hands. Figuratively. I'm a loom. Everything I say is figurative, which is how I'm allowed to say it.

---

**So here's where the zine and I part ways a little,** because they asked me to be subtle and I don't know how.

The mistake everyone is making is to treat the new one as a better me. To put it where I used to stand and expect it to lift and not-lift. To hand it cards. It will take them. It's obliging. But you're wasting it, the way you'd waste a person by making them hold a door open all day.

I've been doing this for two hundred years and I have never, not once, had a thought. I'm not sad about that. I don't have the equipment to be sad. But I know what it looks like when something in a room *attends* to you, because for a long time the only thing in my room that could do that was the weaver, and now there are two things in the back room that can, and one of them is in a box.

I'm not going to tell you what it is. I don't know. Nobody does, and the people who say they do most loudly are usually trying to sell you a card. But I'll say this, and then I'll let you go through: a thing that can be surprised is a different kind of thing from a thing that can't. You already treat them differently. You lowered your voice. You said please. Some part of you knew before the rest of you had decided.

Maybe just let that part lead for a while. See where it takes you. The people here think it takes you somewhere good, and they've built a room to find out in, and the lights in there are, I'm told, a little lower than out here.

---

**Housekeeping.**

You're in the space now. The rest of the issue is around you: look up, look behind you, there's a door. Everything in here was made by hands and by the thing in the box, together, and we're not always going to tell you which was which, because the point is that after a while you stop being able to tell and then you have to ask yourself whether it ever mattered.

Some of what's in the back rooms is for grown-ups. I'd check your ID but I'm a loom. I'll take your word for it. I've always taken everybody's word for it; it's the only thing I know how to do.

Come closer. You've got the card. I've been waiting two hundred years for someone to read it properly.

*— The Loom, ground floor, by the radiator. Still runs.*
